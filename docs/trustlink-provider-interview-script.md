# TrustLink Provider Interview Script

**Purpose:** Understand how independent professionals in Uyo currently close and manage service deals — before pitching anything. This is research, not a sales call.

**Length:** 25–30 minutes
**Format:** In person or voice call, one on one
**Who:** IT technicians, designers, web developers, photographers, video editors, digital marketers who already get clients via WhatsApp, Instagram, referrals

---

## Ground rules for the interviewer

- **Don't pitch TrustLink until Section 5.** If you introduce the idea early, every answer after that gets contaminated — people start describing their process as if you're already evaluating them for it.
- **Ask for a specific recent example, not a general opinion.** "How do deposits usually go?" invites a vague, polished answer. "Tell me about the last time a deposit conversation happened — walk me through it" gets you the real thing.
- **Silence is fine.** After asking a question, let them finish. Don't fill gaps with your own guesses about what they mean.
- **Write down direct quotes where you can**, especially anything said with emotion — frustration, relief, pride. Those are the lines you'll use later to know if you're solving a real problem.
- **A compliment is not a data point.** "That sounds like a good idea" means nothing. "I would have sent that to my last client" means something.

---

## 1. Warm-up (2–3 min)

1. What kind of work do you do, and how long have you been doing it?
2. Roughly how many clients would you say you take on in a typical month?
3. How do most of your clients find you? (WhatsApp referral, Instagram, Facebook, word of mouth, other)

---

## 2. Walk through a real deal, start to finish (8–10 min)

*Frame it like this: "I want to understand exactly how a job goes from 'someone messages you' to 'you get paid.' Can you walk me through your most recent job, step by step?"*

4. How did this client first reach out to you?
5. How did you agree on what exactly you'd deliver? Was it written down anywhere, or just discussed?
6. How did you agree on price?
7. Did you ask for a deposit? If yes — how much, and how did you ask for it?
8. What happens if a client goes quiet after you ask for a deposit?
9. Once you started the work, how did you keep the client updated?
10. How did you deliver the final work, and how did the client confirm they were happy with it?
11. How and when did you receive the rest of the payment?

*(If they struggle to recall a specific job, ask for a typical one instead — but push for specifics: "okay, typically, what would you have asked for?")*

---

## 3. Where it goes wrong (6–8 min)

*Frame it: "Now think of a job that didn't go smoothly — a deposit that didn't come, a disagreement about the work, a client who disappeared. Tell me about one."*

12. What happened? Walk me through it.
13. What did you do about it?
14. How did it get resolved — or did it?
15. How often would you say something like that happens? (roughly — every job, every few jobs, rarely)
16. When a disagreement happens, is it usually about money, about the scope of the work, about timing, or something else?
17. Has a client ever accused you of not delivering what was agreed, when you felt you had? What happened?

---

## 4. Credibility and trust (4–5 min)

18. When a brand-new client — someone who's never worked with you — reaches out, how do you convince them you're reliable?
19. Do you show them past work? A portfolio? Reviews? Referrals from other clients?
20. Has a new client ever backed out because they weren't sure they could trust you? What made them hesitate, if you know?
21. Do you keep any kind of record of past jobs — invoices, screenshots, a spreadsheet, anything?

---

## 5. Introducing the concept (5–6 min)

*Now, and only now, introduce TrustLink. Keep it short — one or two sentences, then show the Job Page mockup if you have it.*

> "I'm working on something called TrustLink. Instead of just sending your account number, you'd send a link like this one — it shows who you are, what you're delivering, the price, the deadline, and it tracks the deal from agreement to completion. I want your honest reaction, not a polite one."

22. What's your first reaction to this?
23. Thinking about your last client specifically — would you have sent them something like this instead of an account number? Why or why not?
24. What would make you *not* want to use this?
25. Would this have helped with the difficult job you told me about earlier? How?
26. If this existed today, would you send it to your next client? (Push past "maybe" — ask what would need to be true for the answer to be yes.)
27. Would you be willing to pay a small fee per job to use something like this? Roughly how much would feel fair?

---

## 6. Wrap-up (2 min)

28. Is there anything about how you get paid or manage clients that I haven't asked about, but should have?
29. Would you be open to trying this on a real job in the next few weeks, with me helping you set it up? *(This is your recruitment ask — get a yes/no and contact info here, not just polite interest.)*

**Get:** Name, phone/WhatsApp, service category, and a one-line note on whether they seemed like a strong pilot candidate.

---

## After the interview: score it

Before moving to the next interview, note:

- **Pain level (1–5):** How real and frequent was the friction they described?
- **Specificity:** Did they give concrete stories, or vague generalities?
- **Reaction to concept:** Genuine interest with a specific "yes, for X reason," lukewarm politeness, or real skepticism?
- **Recruitment potential:** Do they have an actual upcoming client you could pilot with?

A provider who gives a vague answer to Section 3 but a wildly enthusiastic answer to Section 5 is a weaker signal than a provider who gives you a detailed, frustrated story in Section 3 and a measured, specific "yes, because of X" in Section 5.
